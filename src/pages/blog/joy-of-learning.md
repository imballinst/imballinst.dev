Learning is a process of knowing something. There are levels to it: knowing, doing, teaching, and maybe some in-betweens. I have learned that knowing a skill does not necessarily translate into having mastered that skill. It may just be a "good to know" thing and I'll get on with my life.

Not everything can be learned while having fun. Of course, it is best to have both. It is better if the learning is painful but you can still have fun; it may be annoying if the learning is easy but you aren't having fun. Yes, yes, it is a nightmare if the learning process is hard AND you aren't having fun. I find mathematics fun but only if I see someone explaining it to me. If I have to do it alone, it will be a disaster.

I find learning fun when the dopamine hits. Solving a problem, identifying root causes, finding common threads, detecting patterns. If there is no "end", unfortunately that is when it stops providing fun to me, if there is any to begin with. There are, however, instances where the dopamine hits are just simply "delayed", as with my latest hobby: gardening. But before we get to that, I want to first share the learnings that I think are memorable.

## Memorable things learned

I'd say there are a lot of memorable things that I have learned so far in my life, but if I have to limit them just so this doesn't become a worse wall of text:

### Learning to play strategy games

I was in elementary school; I don't think I played a lot of games back then other than things like Solitaire, Pinball, and Hearts (which until now I have no idea how it works, btw). My brother taught me about Counter-Strike, Command & Conquer games, and Warcraft III. We played against AI (because back then we didn't have internet), and that was how we got a bit better at the games.

### Learning to play strategy games against real people

I remember back then I went to an internet cafe with my brother and played Red Alert 2 against other folks in the cafe. I think that was my first experience playing against other people, so the usual "textbook" tactics that worked against AI of course didn't work against them. My brother was mad at me at that time because we lost (xd) and our parents scolded me because I wasn't supposed to go to an internet cafe (I was around 12 years old back then).

### Learning to socialize

I was super bad at socializing back then. I had some connections through Indogamers' Battle.net Warcraft III server, interacted a lot in the forums (🕊️), and we even met up in real life. I remember I was a very... "steerable" person back then, maybe I still am. When I had different thoughts from someone I trusted, I just followed them instead of following what I really thought. I didn't realize this until 2018 when one of my colleagues told me that I shouldn't be a "yes person". From then, I tried to... learn how to still be able to socialize while keeping a strong stance.

I _think_ my social skills have improved a bit as well since I became a manager... because like, I was _forced_ to do 1:1s with my direct reports, so eventually I learned more about them and I could adapt my "persona" to each of them better as time went on.

### Learning software engineering

This was probably what shaped me the most into the person I am today (for better or worse). I initially chose [Informatics](https://stei.itb.ac.id/redesign/en/program-sarjana/sarjana-informatika/) because I liked playing games (foolish thought, I know). ### Learning family dynamics

After I got married, it was pretty eye-opening for me because I had to compromise with my wife (and vice versa) to "discard" bad things that our parents' family taught us and "create" new ones that align with our values. We both didn't really have a "good blueprint" so we had to navigate ourselves... and sometimes with the help of our trusted psychologist.

While we are at it, never trust the "sunshine and rainbows" family stories from your friends or celebrities. Maybe they are the same behind the curtain, or maybe they aren't. Make your own "north star" with your spouse, because you both are different from others.

### Learning politics

I used to be apathetic (I am not proud of it). I thought every government was just equally garbage so I stayed put. The first 2 are currently on their last legs (I wanted to save the seeds) and the last 2 are still ongoing. But, how does it really relate to the overall theme of this post, really?

## The importance of slowing down

Back then, when we wanted to do research, we _still_ had the time to do the research. When we discovered the thing that we looked for, that was when the dopamine hit. We felt excited; we felt satisfied.

However, now, with Generative AI making everything faster, that research time is reduced _significantly_. The dopamine hit no longer happens when solving something at the code level, but rather at the product level (for better or worse). We no longer get the "reward" from deep focus on _translating_ what we think into code, seeing the tests passing, and the functionality working. The existence of Generative AI also raises expectations that everything has to be done faster [in terms of speed] and bigger [in terms of volume]. In software engineering, we could see how a lot of people "gloat" over their 1000 commits/PRs in a week.

<details>
  <summary>Why commit and PR numbers are bad metrics</summary>

Back then, before Generative AI, there were often discourses about "what makes a software engineer considered productive". We used all kinds of measures: how long they were in the office, how long they were being "active" in applications such as Slack/Teams, how many pull requests they produced daily, and so on. The majority agreed that these metrics were vanity at best.

But now, with Generative AI, people somehow bring this discourse again. The difference is, it's not them who push the pull requests, but rather agents. Tokenmaxxers (including top folks in frontier AI labs) learned how review becomes the bottleneck, so they came up with "bandaids", such as agents reviewing agents. Here lie my other 2 concerns: (1) not everyone has "unlimited" tokens to review that volume of contributions, and (2) not everyone has "unlimited" compute/money to cover the CI which ensures the pull request won't cause build-time or even runtime errors.

Software engineering is often called out because we don't care about performance, e.g. desktop apps being made in Electron which eats a lot of memory. It amazes me that it is not being called out for the two cases of "overspending" above. Top folks in frontier AI labs, who I respect for their impressive competency in the industry, don't really touch the ground. Not all companies can equal their expenditures. I don't think they care either about the impact of Generative AI on industries other than software engineering, which is very unfortunate.
</details>

For folks who have been at it for years, maybe it is fine, because we have the experience for it. But if you ask a "fresh me" going through this era and my job is to prompt LLMs to complete a task, I will get the impression of using [an answer key](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/answer_key). I might understand it _when I read it_, but I don't fully internalize it. How could I internalize it when the demand to go faster is more than ever?

For perspective, usually in MMORPG world-first race (e.g. World of Warcraft, Final Fantasy XIV), raid groups will have additional people (let's call them "analysts") on top of their core player group who usually streams their point of view (PoV). This is needed because players are actually required to not only do the mechanics, but they also need to do damage to beat the encounter.

Based on players' PoV, the analysts will collect as much information as possible and provide it to the group, usually when the group is currently hitting a wall (e.g. a complex mechanic). Sometimes, maybe the analysts aren't needed since the players can already put two and two together on how to solve the mechanics, but still, _it is very rare_ that they can beat a difficult fight on their first try. After the group has collected vital information about a mechanic, they usually take a step back and discuss on how to resolve it. This is how they _internalize_ what is going to happen, and this is also how they get so good at the game, that if a similar mechanic happens in the future in another encounter, their mind will ring a bell.

Unfortunately, the same doesn't apply to tech nowadays. The popular mantra "it depends" doesn't apply when it comes to the discussion around Generative AI. When do we use Generative AI? When do we not use it? What happens if we use it blindly? What is the impact on other industries? What is the impact on those working informal jobs?

## My latest learning: gardening

![My basil plant, before it flowered.](/assets/blog/joy-of-learning/basil.jpeg "size=400w")

I mentioned that I learned gardening recently. I have to say, if you told me in the past that I would learn gardening in the future, I'd say, _"it's fake news"_. I have a soft spot for pets (rabbits and cats), but plants... not really. See, before that, I had this wild idea to take a vacation to a mountain-ish area (which I haven't yet realized until now). I thought it would be cool to, you know, touch grass, reconnect with nature, and so on. I always thought that soil was dirty and I am a bit averse to dirtying my hands. Hell, even when cooking I wash my hands every few steps 😅. That is, until I learned how to grow a new plant from kitchen scraps.

From there, it was a straight-up rabbit hole. I found some great YouTube channels around gardening and composting, such as [GrowVeg](https://www.youtube.com/@GrowVeg) and [SheffieldMadeGardens](https://www.youtube.com/@SheffieldMadeGardens)... and then I just started getting some gardening things. I bought some discount [bayam seeds (or amaranthus)](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayam), put them in a plastic cup container filled with potting mix, and surprisingly, it gave me a new, refreshing routine every day. I have an excuse to step away from the screen and take a look at how my plants are doing.

As a software engineer whose life wrestles with basically binary numbers, I find gardening to be almost the opposite side of it. Sure, there are "formulas" on how to [min-max](https://www.dictionary.com/browse/min-max) the yield, but at the end of the day, every gardener's approach may differ from one another. The climate may be different, wind level may be different, temperature level may be different, planting space may be different... all that comes down to problem solving with constraints. The last one is the most interesting one for me. Since I live in an apartment (with a balcony), I don't have _that_ much space like the YouTube gardeners with tons of garden spaces. So, I have to make the most of my balcony space to plant the things... I think people usually call it "urban farming".

![My basil plant's flowers, which turn brown when pollinated; the seeds can be extracted.](/assets/blog/joy-of-learning/basil-flower.jpeg "size=400w")

![My indoor plants, which I shower with a grow light.](/assets/blog/joy-of-learning/indoor-grow-light.jpeg "size=400w")

How does this correlate to learning? So I happened to also learn about [grow lights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grow_light), which I also use for seedlings before they are transplanted outside on the balcony. Since my apartment unit only effectively gets direct sunlight from like 1pm to 5pm, I need to compensate for it, because from what I learned, it seems like peppers in particular really need light to grow strong, especially when they're still seedlings. That brings us to another rabbit hole!

_"Wait, does that mean I can just leave my grow light on for 24/7 and my plant will grow extra fast?"_ Unfortunately, no, because [it will cause stress to the plants](https://theonegrow.com.au/can-you-leave-grow-lights-on-24-7-schedule/). Which brings us back to the "learning" and "internalizing". If grow lights can "supercharge" a plant's growth, it will eventually hit an "effectiveness" peak and turn south if kept longer. Plants need rest, too. Same for humans, Generative AI may help us "supercharge" our work, but if you don't take a step back, stop, and learn what you have prompted, then at some point it _might_ reduce your productivity.

## Closing words

So, yeah, there's that. Personally, I still enjoy learning things that I can be good at. I won't say I am good at gardening (yet), but seeing how I can consume the fresh produce... it gives me heart. It may take time; it may be slow compared to what I have experienced in software development using Generative AI, but I still believe in internalization and mindfulness.
