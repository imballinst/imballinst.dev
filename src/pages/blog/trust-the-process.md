December 20, 2019. Mikel Arteta began his first-ever managerial career at Arsenal, who was 10th place on the Premier League table. Arteta had this philosophy about "build from the back", which Manchester City at that time also expressed in their games. However, due to a lot of Arsenal players were not very technical, this philosophy sometimes became a boomerang. Players considered passing to teammates as a way to "get this danger away from me", which was frustrating to watch as a fan, because passing sideways became the "safe action".

Against the odds, he beat Manchester City and Chelsea, both 2-1, in the semi-finals and finals of the FA Cup, getting him the first trophy, despite finishing 8th in the Premier League.

## Arsenal's seasons

### 2020-21 season

Arsenal started their 2020/21 season with a Community Shield match against Liverpool, who won the previous Premier League season in a flashy way, blitzing and clinching the title with just 7 games to go (86 points: 31 played, 28 won, 2 drew, 1 lost). One could have thought they would be able to score 100 points, but they came close with just 99 points. Keep in mind that was the 2nd time they finished the league **second time in a row**, with the previous season they got 97 points _and still lost_ the title to Manchester City, who had 98 points.

Now that I glazed Liverpool enough already, let's get back to the Community Shield. Long story short, Arsenal started their season with a Community Shield over a penalty shootout after the match ended with a 1-1 draw after extra time.

Arsenal's season, however, riddled with many struggles in the process. At one point, they dropped to the 15th position after 14 games. It was a rough patch of games, almost one and half months without a win: Aston Villa (H) 0-3, Leeds United (A) 0-0, Wolves (H) 1-2, Spurs (A) 2-0, Burnley (H) 0-1, Southampton (H) 1-1. Fans were beginning calling out the sacking of Arteta. You could feel the "toxicity" in the stadium, which rightly so, because the players didn't turn up to the levels that they were expected to. Bukayo Saka, at that time one of the Arsenal's starboy from Hale End graduate, even apologized to the fans.

With Arsenal being unstable across the season, qualification to European leagues were outside of their grasp. For the first time in almost 25 years, Arsenal would not compete in Europe, despite salvaging 5-win streak at the end of the season. They finished 8th, again, behind Spurs. Again, fans were calling out for Arteta for improvements (or for sacking). But, the Arsenal owners had another idea. Josh Kroenke, the son of Stan Kroenke, began to be more involved in the club. And thus the painful process of resurgence began.

### 2021-22 season

Arsenal brought some players who, until the time of writing, are still part of the core team: Ben White and Martin Ødegaard. Honorable mentions to Aaron Ramsdale and Takehiro Tomiyasu, who quickly became fan favorites during their time at Arsenal.

Despite the signings, Arsenal's luck turned out to be the same as before. Getting bullied by Brentford on the opening day, which some commentators just brushed aside the act of holding out goalkeepers during corners, telling the goalkeeper to "be stronger" (spoilers: they were crying when Arsenal did the same in later seasons). It didn't end there. Arsenal continued to lose against Chelsea (0-2) and Manchester City (5-0), putting them at the bottom table with 0 points, 0 goals scored, 9 goals conceded.

Arsenal were looking to secure their Champions League qualification since 2015/16, but they fell short at the end as they lost 2 of the last 3 games. Yet again, another trophyless season. Yet again, fans voiced their concerns. And yet again, Josh still backed up Mikel.

### 2022-23 season

Gabriel Jesus and Oleksandr Zinchenko were brought in from Manchester City and they transformed Arsenal. They revived the electricity in the stadium. They revived the hope in the fans. Fans dared to dream a dream they didn't even dare to think before: the Premier League title. Further additions coming in during the January transfer, particularly to strenghten ARsenal's attack because of the injury suffered by Jeus in the World Cup. The most important player brought in was Leandro Trossard, who was very prolific in terms of goals and assists.

And yet, Arsenal fell short. Again. Until matchweek 33, they were 1st. Matchweek 34 and onwards, they were 2nd, with losses against Brighton and Nottingham sealed Arsenal's second place on the table. Another trophyless season.

### 2023-24 season

Despite the trophyless season, the fans got more hope now. There were not as much "Arteta Out" brigade as previous seasons. Arsenal bought 3 players who until the time of writing, are still the key part of the team: Kai Havertz, Jurrien Timber, and Declan Rice. Unlike the 2022-23 where Arsenal spent most of the time top of the table, this season's trials and tribulations were clearer, particularly because the injuries. Gabriel Jesus, who were injured a year ago in the World Cup, got injured again, robbing Arsenal of one of the key players who made them electric.

The silver lining? It forced Arteta to make Havertz as a Center Forward (CF). He was massive in the title run-in. Out of 18 remaining games in 2024, Arsenal won 16, drew 1, lost 1, with the lost being against Aston Villa 0-2 at home. Arsenal were behind Manchester City by 2 points when the matchweek 38 started. There were faint hope in the eyes of the fans, as they were hoping West Ham could win or drew against Manchester City at the Etihad Stadium. _If_ that happened and Arsenal won, Arsenal would win the title by goal difference.

Alas, it was not meant to be as Manchester City won 3-1. The 2 points gap remained and Manchester City clinched the title on the final day. There were discomfort amongst the fans. This was supposed to be Arsenal's season. They had everything lining up during the run-in. As unfortunate as the injury to Gabriel Jesus, Kai Havertz were able to produce a good output. They didn't have as many injuries as they would normally have. And yet, they fell short, including in Champions League where they were knocked out in quarter-finals 3-2 against Bayern Munich.

The fans were wondering if, another manager would be able to bring Arsenal across the finish line.

### 2024-25 season

I didn't really want to talk about this season because there were so much bull\*\*\*\* in it. Rice's second yellow card against Brighton at home, Trossard's second yellow card against City away, and Myles-Lewis Skelly red card against Wolves away, all that contributed to injuries because some players had to cover more grounds because of they were down a player.

Arsenal fans were wondering if Arteta's training sessions were too hard on the players, causing them to be more injury prone. This was especially moreso because during the Dubai getaway, Havertz was injured. With Jesus, Martinelli, and Saka were also injured at that time, Arsenal's options were limited for the center forward, with Raheem Sterling being one of them.

And yet, there was always a silver lining. Away to Leicester City, Arteta brought Mikel Merino as a substitute to Raheem Sterling, who got to the right place, right time, with cool, calm, and composed finishes. Twice. While Liverpool were running away with the league (and rightly so), Arsenal aimed Champions League as their last hope, as unlikely as it was, since they had to contend with Real Madrid.

The magic of the cup, they said. No one had it in their bingo that the home leg against Real Madrid would be Declan Rice's show, with Merino's goal as cherry on top.

### 2025-26 season

Finally, the rest is history. Arsenal felt like they were so close to the title, so they brought in some more players: Martin Zubimendi, Christian Nørgaard, Noni Madueke, Christian Mosquera, Viktor Gyokeres, and Eberechi Eze, which added a veritable of squad depth to the team. The issue that hindered them in previous seasons, injuries, would hopefully be avoidable with the squad depth.

Arsenal started the season with flying colors, as if they went through the 2022-23 season again. They were 1st since matchweek 7 and City had been giving chase since then. At times, Arsenal became complacent and suffered some nasty winless streak, such as Liverpool 0-0 at home, Nottingham 0-0 away, and Manchester United 2-3 home. Some times later, they also had 2 draws in a row, 1-1 away against Brentford and 2-2 away against Wolves.

After every bad patch, Arsenal always bounced back with a series of wins. Things were looking bleak at matchweek 32, where they lost 1-2 at home to Bournemouth. The following week, they would travel to Etihad to play City, who had a game in hand. Arsenal lost 2-1 and City won their game in hand. For the first time since matchweek 7, City claimed the first place on the table.

And yet... this time, for real, the stars aligned for Arsenal. Win streaks against Newcastle 1-0 at home, Fulham 3-0 at home, West Ham 0-1 away, and finally 1-0 Burnley, gave Arsenal the guarantee they needed to win the title. In the same time range, City drew against Everton 3-3 away and Bournemouth 1-1 away. Finally, after 22 years, Arsenal were able to lift the Premier League trophy.

It was great, but it was almost perfect, had it not been for the lost in the Champions League final against, again, Paris Saint-Germain. But then again, a clear progress was being made. Quarter-finals, semi-finals, finals. Second, second, second, first. With the Premier League title secured, I reckon it was as if, a huge burden had been lifted from every member of the Arsenal FC.

## What's the takeaway?!

All that blabber above, for what?! For this: lately, I saw posts in the software engineering industry that say things like (paraphrased): _"Software engineers have a hard time using AI agents because they are not accustomed to outcome-oriented (building a product)"_. I think it's bulls\*\*\*. If you are always outcome-oriented, then you'd fire Arteta before he won the Premier League title, because you only look at the results and not on the details.

I don't disagree that the goal of a software engineer is to build a good product. Good here being in good UX-wise, humanizes its users, and is reliable. That is why I am not entirely opposed to AI-assisted development, because I see value in it and I _can_ feel empowered because of it. At least, in software engineering. I still hold my ground that AI art is ass.

However, when a leader says something-something "outcome-oriented", it just leaves a bad taste in my mouth. We always talk about the result, the outcome, yada-yada. But we often forget about the process, who do the work, which is the people. Have we given them a good example? Have we given them the incentives and space to grow so they can be "outcome-oriented"? Have we given them the space they need to take a step back, rest, and recover?

Let's say we have these code blocks. Both have the same result, and theoretically, outcome.

```js
console.log("a\nb\nc\nd\ne")

["abcde"].split("").forEach(c => console.log(c))
```

If we are too outcome oriented and don't care about the process, we will probably be whatever to the differences above. But, at some point, those past processes that we have been ignoring will come to bite us back at some point. _"Why did we decide with using split when we can just simply log one time with newlines?"_ It's a very simplified and out-of-the-world example, I'm sure, but I think every one of us who cares about the process and have done AI-assisted development already experienced this before, and in turn, add rules/instructions to a Markdown file so that future agents won't repeat it again. Those who don't care about the process won't care, as long as the outcome is correct.

People always say that AI makes us more productive in software engineering. But we rarely talk about _how much brain work_ that we have to do in a day. All that contributes to this... "outcome-oriented" product. But at what cost? Sure, there are a lot of people who are "outcome-oriented" can withstand years of compacted 9-5 work. But it's not realistic if that expectation is applied to everyone in an instant, especially without proper incentives.

It reminds me of True Sight of TI9, where Kuro told MC to play like an opposing player. It _felt_ disrespectful because it was as if MC had to "become" another player at that time. It was the outcome that Kuro wanted, but morale-wise, it wasn't wise to say so. Or at least, as a user in the YouTube video comment section wrote, there were other better choice words to use.

> Kuro: "I just need MC to play like 7ckngMad (Ceb)."
> MC: "And what does he do?"

So, yeah. In summary:

- Thank you Josh Kroenke for not giving in to media narratives about Mikel Arteta, Arsenal finally won their first Premier League title in 22 years.
- Leaders see AI as a shortcut for their product (and rightfully so, since it's their business). However, I'd like to see them talk more about the process and the people. It's not good to tunnel vision outcome, because we lose sight of things that made it happen.
