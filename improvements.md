**Notes for the author:**
- Three of the 24 fixes this round (rows 6, 10, 17) were broken clause-attachment sentences where a relative pronoun or non-restrictive clause had no clear antecedent and the surrounding clauses couldn't be parsed. Worth a self-check pass that focuses specifically on sentences where you have a long participial phrase followed by a "which/who/that" — confirm the pronoun actually refers to something.
- A cluster of wrong-preposition / missing-preposition slips (rows 2, 7, 15, 16, 19, 24) suggests these would benefit from a separate read focused only on the prepositions inside phrasal verbs and idioms ("look at", "decide on", "tunnel vision on", "in Xth place", "at the bottom of").

## Flow and content suggestions

These are structural and flow-level suggestions, not line-edits. The goal is to make the article's point land harder, not to polish prose.

### TL;DR of the suggestions

1. The football narrative and the thesis stay disconnected until the very end — the connection has to be inferred for most of the piece. (high)
2. "What's the takeaway?!" bundles four distinct sub-arguments under one heading with thin transitions between them. (medium)
3. The "In summary" bullets only restate two of the threads the takeaway section develops; several are left out. (medium)
4. The 2024-25 Spurs tangent (Europa League win + near-relegation) doesn't tie back to the main thread and breaks the build-up to the title payoff. (medium)

### 1. The football narrative and the thesis stay disconnected until the very end (high)

- **Issue:** From "December 20, 2019" through the end of the 2025-26 season section, the body is pure football recap — transfers, scorelines, injuries, red cards, league standings — with no reference to software engineering, AI, or "process vs. outcome" as a framing. The "What's the takeaway?!" heading is where the explicit bridge to the SE/AI argument appears, which means a reader has to hold six seasons of detail in mind before learning why any of it matters.
- **Impact:** A reader looking for the process/outcome argument (which is what the title and description promise) may bounce off the long recap before reaching the payoff, or read the whole recap without a lens to organize it by. Either way, the central claim lands as a punchline rather than as the spine of the piece.
- **Suggestions:**
  1. Open with a short framing paragraph before the season-by-season recap that states the process/outcome lens up front, so each season is read through that lens as it happens rather than retrofitted at the end.
  2. Drop brief callback lines into two or three of the season sections (e.g., after the 2021-22 "bottom of the table" low point, after the 2023-24 final-day collapse) that explicitly tie the moment to the process argument, so the connection builds gradually instead of arriving all at once.
  3. Trim the recap to the two or three seasons that most directly demonstrate "trusting the process despite bad outcomes" (the 2021-22 rock-bottom start and the 2025-26 title) and compress the others to a paragraph or two each, shortening the distance to the thesis.

### 2. "What's the takeaway?!" bundles several distinct sub-arguments (medium)

- **Issue:** Under one heading, the section moves through: the Arteta/firing analogy (L107), a personal stance on AI-assisted development with an "AI art is ass" aside (L109), a code example (L113-121), a rhetorical question about giving engineers space and incentives (L111), a burnout/brain-work argument (L123), and the AI-rules-in-markdown-files aside (L121) — each a self-contained point with only light connective tissue between them.
- **Impact:** The core claim (leaders should value process, not just outcome) gets diluted across five or six sub-topics, making it harder for a reader to walk away with one clear takeaway rather than a loose cluster of related opinions.
- **Suggestions:**
  1. Split the section into two subheadings — one for the process argument itself (Arteta analogy + code example) and one for its cost to engineers (burnout/incentives + AI-rules-in-markdown aside) — so the two threads don't compete for the reader's attention in one block.
  2. Cut or shorten the "AI art is ass" one-liner, since it's a personal stance that doesn't advance the process argument and currently sits as an unexplained aside.
  3. Add a one-sentence transition before the code example explicitly linking it back to the "outcome-oriented" critique, so the example arrives as a demonstration of the prior paragraph's point rather than as a fresh thought.

### 3. The "In summary" bullets don't cover most of the takeaway section's threads (medium)

- **Issue:** The three summary bullets only restate the Arteta/Kroenke point (L127) and two generic AI/leadership points (L128-129). They don't mention the code example, the burnout/brain-work argument, the "add rules/instructions to a Markdown file" pattern, or the AI-art aside — all of which appear in "What's the takeaway?!" and take up real space.
- **Impact:** Readers skimming the summary for the article's full argument will miss several ideas the author spent paragraphs developing, making the ending feel narrower than the body.
- **Suggestions:**
  1. Add a bullet (or fold into an existing one) that captures the burnout / brain-work point, since it's arguably the most concrete claim in the section about the human cost of process-ignoring leadership.
  2. If the code example is meant as an illustration rather than an independent point, say so explicitly near the summary (e.g., "as the `console.log` vs. `.split()` example showed...") so its omission from the bullets doesn't read as an oversight.
  3. Shorten the summary's scope intentionally by retitling it (e.g., "Two things I want you to take from this") so it's clear the bullets are a deliberate distillation, not a full recap.

### 4. The 2024-25 Spurs tangent doesn't tie back to the main thread (medium)

- **Issue:** L89-91 is a three-paragraph excursion about Spurs winning the Europa League while finishing 16th-17th, then nearly getting relegated the following season. The point seems to be "even winning a trophy didn't satisfy the owner; they sacked the manager anyway, and it got worse" — but this connection to the "trust the process" thesis is left implicit, and the "spoiler" near-relegation aside adds another sentence of Spurs content that doesn't cash in either.
- **Impact:** The Spurs tangent lands right before the climactic 2025-26 title run-in, so it pushes the payoff farther away and forces the reader to do extra work to connect it to anything. A casual reader may just experience it as a digression and lose the throughline of the recap.
- **Suggestions:**
  1. Cut the entire Spurs tangent and use that space to give the 2024-25 season itself more breathing room — the season's injuries, red cards, and Merino-substitution goals deserve more narrative weight before the title win anyway.
  2. If the Spurs material is kept, make the connection to the thesis explicit in one sentence (e.g., "And yet the Spurs example shows the opposite failure mode: an owner who looked at the trophy and forgot the league position still ended up sacked when the league position kept slipping") so the reader doesn't have to infer it.
  3. Either remove the "I didn't really want to talk about this season" meta-commentary (L77) or reframe it as part of the process argument — e.g., "this season had plenty of moments that tested the process" — instead of authorial dissatisfaction that breaks the narrative voice.

## What's working well (don't touch)

- The season-by-season structure is genuinely well organized — each section has a clear arc (setback → response → outcome) that mirrors the "trust the process" theme even before it's named, and the recurring "yet again, fans voiced their concerns" / "yet again, Josh still backed Mikel" cadence gives the body a satisfying rhythm.
- The voice is distinctive and consistent throughout: casual asides ("Now that I glazed Liverpool enough already"), rhetorical questions, fragments for emphasis, and the occasional profanity all read as intentional and give the piece personality without crowding out the argument.
- The `console.log("a\nb\nc\nd\ne")` vs. `["abcde"].split("").forEach(...)` code example is a strong, concrete illustration of "same outcome, different process" — it's the clearest bridge to the software-engineering point in the whole piece and is worth keeping front and center in the takeaway section.
- The Josh Kroenke thread (introduced quietly in 2020-21, paid off across 2021-22 / 2022-23 / 2025-26) is a well-executed long-range setup and payoff — it gives the recap a throughline beyond just match results and is exactly the kind of "trust the process in the people" structure the article argues for.
